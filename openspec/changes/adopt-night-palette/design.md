## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:**
- One palette across the whole site: night, wine and champagne on cream.
- A test that fails if a saturated token drifts back towards the reference.

**Non-Goals:**
- The world's materials, and new layout. See the proposal.

## Decisions

### D1 - Roles, not a find-and-replace

| Was | Now | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `bg-teal-deep`, hover `bg-teal-dark` | `bg-night`, hover `bg-wine` | filled primary buttons |
| `text-teal` (script, links) | `text-night`; `text-wine` for script accents | pale surfaces |
| `text-araucaria`, `bg-araucaria` | `text-night`, `bg-night` | headings, deep bands |
| `ring-teal/40` | `ring-wine/35` | focus on pale surfaces |
| gold `#E2B04A` | champagne `#D9BF86` | same roles |

Hover moving from night to wine, rather than to a darker night, gives the button a
visible reply on the dark end of the palette, where darkening shows nothing.

*Alternative considered:* keep the token names and change their values (`--color-teal`
set to night). Rejected: a token called teal that renders blue lies to whoever reads the
next class name.

### D2 - Champagne, `#D9BF86`

It stands 0.088 from the reference mustard, and night on champagne reaches 8.5:1.
Brighter golds all stayed within 0.08 of the mustard: `#E3BD6B` at 0.062, `#D8A950` at
0.055. Navy, burgundy and champagne is a combination the reference's orange, teal and
mustard shares nothing with.

### D3 - The place card takes its place's colour

The card gets `placeTheme(place.accent)`:
- a band in the accent carrying "Bem-vindo a" (Yellowtail, wash colour) and the name
  (Figtree 800, mist);
- the tagline in `--place-ink`;
- "Visitar" filled in the accent, hovering to `--place-deep`.

The card and the page it opens are then one colour, so the iris transition opens onto the
colour the visitor was already looking at. Every accent passes 4.5:1 with mist (tested in
`elevate-place-pages`).

### D4 - Pins

- The fill is `--color-gold`, which is now champagne, and the glyph is `--color-night`.
- The offer pill stays wine.
- The drop shadow becomes night at 35%, not the bark-brown `rgba`.

### D5 - The wordmark's corner marks

`logo.png` is a card with a fleuron in each corner. The crop in `build-logo.py` cuts the
frame line but keeps the fleurons' inner tips, and `emblem_mask` keeps them because they
are saturated. The mask now keeps only its large connected regions: a region smaller than
0.5% of the largest is paper, not emblem. The labelling is a breadth-first fill in pure
Python on the reduced mask (about 240,000 pixels), because the machine has no numpy.
Both files are rebuilt with `npm run build:logo`.

### D6 - The guard

`tests/palette.test.ts` reads the `@theme` block of `globals.css`, computes each token's
OKLab chroma, and holds every token above 0.04 to at least 0.08 from each reference
swatch. It also fails if the interface source still names a retired token.

## Implementation notes

- **D5:** the first criterion tried was an island's bounding box against the emblem's.
  The banner's tail reaches the bottom-right corner, so one mark survived. The one used
  is distance: a small island with no pixel within 14px of the emblem is dropped.
  Measured on the reduced masks, the corner tips lie beyond 14px and every part of the
  emblem, the banner's letters included, within 10px.
- **D5:** the rebuilt logo showed no change on screen until Next's image cache was emptied.
  The cache keys on URL. `build-logo.py` now empties it itself, as `images:sharpen` does.
- **Retired too:** the four `--color-sky-*` tokens. They were unused, and two sat on the
  reference's coral and mustard. The 3D sky has its own constants.

## Risks / Trade-offs

- [Champagne pins are paler than gold ones over the sunlit terrain] → The night glyph and
  the drop shadow carry the contrast. Checked in a capture of the map.
- [The crest files change colour again: the rim becomes champagne] → The files are
  regenerated by the script, not edited by hand.
